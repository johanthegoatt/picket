Picket

Say how long a fence you want and it gives you the panels, posts, postmix, gravel boards and caps to buy, and how long the posts need to be.

How it works

Fence panels sit in bays between posts, so the number of panels is your run divided by one panel's width, rounded up, and you need one more post than panels because there's a post at each end as well as between every panel. Corners, gates and a run that ends against a wall each add a post, so there's a box for extras.

A post is set about a third of its height into the ground, roughly 600 mm for a full-height fence, so a 1.83 m (6 ft) fence wants a 2.4 m post. Postmix — the rapid-set bagged concrete you pour in dry and wet down — is usually 1 to 2 bags a post in firm ground and 2 to 3 in soft, a bit more for a tall or heavy-duty post, so the count is shown as a range.

Panel widths vary by make: a 6 ft panel is 1.83 m but European panels are 1.80 m, and over a long boundary the difference adds up, so check the panel before you buy the posts. The method and figures follow Jacksons Fencing's fence builder and Wern-Wood's panels, posts and postmix guide.

Picket is a shopping-list guide, not a structural design. Deeper posts, concrete spurs or a wind-rated fence may be needed on an exposed or high boundary, and a boundary fence can have rules about height and ownership. Check with your supplier or a fencer if you're unsure.
